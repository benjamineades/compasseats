## PART 1 — SOURCES ON THE SITE TODAY

### Michelin Guide
The oldest and most famous restaurant rating in the world, published since the early 1900s by — yes, really — the French tire company, which originally wanted motorists driving farther and wearing out more rubber. It works the same way today as it always has: anonymous, professionally trained inspectors book under fake names, pay their own bills, and return multiple times before judging. Stars are awarded for the food alone — quality of ingredients, mastery of technique, harmony of flavors, and consistency — never for the décor or the fame of the chef. A single star can transform a restaurant's fortunes overnight, which is why chefs the world over both chase and fear this guide.

**Categories:**
- **Three Stars ★★★** — Michelin's own definition: "exceptional cuisine, worth a special journey." In practice, get on a plane. Only around 150 restaurants worldwide hold it at any moment.
- **Two Stars ★★** — "Excellent cooking, worth a detour." Refined, deeply skilled kitchens one step from the summit.
- **One Star ★** — "High-quality cooking, worth a stop." Sounds modest until you learn most chefs work a lifetime without earning one.
- **Bib Gourmand** — Michelin's value award: genuinely good cooking at a moderate price. No stars, no formality — it's where the inspectors eat on their day off.
- **Selected (The Plate)** — Restaurants the inspectors recommend without a star or Bib. Simply appearing in the Guide means Michelin vouches for it.
- **Green Star** — A separate distinction for restaurants leading on sustainability, from sourcing to waste.

### World's 50 Best Restaurants
An annual ranked list of the fifty best restaurants on Earth, published since 2002 and voted on by an academy of more than 1,000 chefs, food writers, and well-traveled gourmets around the world, each naming the best meals of their recent travels. Where Michelin rewards precision and consistency, 50 Best captures momentum — the restaurants defining where food is going right now. The list is revealed at an annual ceremony with genuine Oscar-night energy, and a spot near the top fills a reservation book for roughly the rest of recorded time.

**Categories:**
- **Top 50 (ranked)** — The main event: fifty restaurants ranked No. 1 to No. 50 by academy votes.
- **51–100** — The extended list just below the cut. "Only" the 73rd best restaurant on the planet is still a remarkable place to have dinner.

**Regional editions (same voting model, regional academies):**
- **Asia's 50 Best Restaurants** — Published since 2013; the authority on the continent's most exciting dining, from Tokyo counters to Bangkok tasting menus.
- **Latin America's 50 Best Restaurants** — Also since 2013, covering the region that produced some of this century's most influential kitchens.
- **Middle East & North Africa's 50 Best Restaurants** — Launched in 2022, right as the region's dining scene stopped being a secret.
- **North America's 50 Best Restaurants** — The newest restaurant edition, bringing the 50 Best voting model to North America.

### World's 50 Best Bars
The definitive annual ranking of the world's greatest cocktail bars, published since 2009 and run by the same organization behind World's 50 Best Restaurants. Hundreds of bartenders, drinks writers, and dedicated regulars vote on the best bar experiences of their year — grand hotel institutions, ten-seat hidden dens, and everything in between. A place on this list means the drinks are worth crossing a city for, and quite possibly an ocean.

**Categories:**
- **Top 50 (ranked)** — The fifty best bars in the world, ranked by academy votes. The top ten become pilgrimage sites more or less overnight.
- **51–100** — The extended ranking: bars knocking loudly on the door of the top 50.

**Regional editions:**
- **Asia's 50 Best Bars** — Published since 2016, covering the region that turned Singapore and Hong Kong into cocktail capitals.
- **North America's 50 Best Bars** — Launched in 2022 for the US, Canada, Mexico, and the Caribbean.
- **North America's 50 Best Bars (51–100)** — The extended North American ranking.
- **Asia's 50 Best Bars (51–100)** — The extended Asian ranking.
- **Europe's 50 Best Bars** — The newest regional list, debuting in 2026 with its first ceremony in Amsterdam — the continent that invented much of bar culture finally getting its own scoreboard.

### James Beard Awards
America's most prestigious food honors, presented since 1991 by the foundation named for James Beard, the cookbook author who spent a lifetime insisting American cooking deserved to be taken seriously. Often called the Oscars of American food — and unlike most things given that label, these come with actual medallions. Restaurants, chefs, and bars across the US are honored through a multi-round process of nominations and votes by a large body of independent judges. A Beard medallion by the door remains one of the surest signs in America that you've chosen well.

**Categories:**
- **Outstanding Restaurant** — The top honor for a single restaurant demonstrating excellence over many years, with no sign of stopping.
- **Outstanding Chef** — The highest individual honor in American cooking; career-defining is underselling it.
- **Best Chef (by region)** — Honors the finest chef in each region of the US — very often the award that turns a local favorite into a national reservation problem.
- **Outstanding Bar** — Recognizes a bar with exceptional drinks, hospitality, and atmosphere, judged as seriously as any kitchen.
- **Best New Restaurant** — The country's most impressive opening of the past year.
- **America's Classics** — Beloved, often family-run institutions with timeless appeal — the diners, barbecue joints, and neighborhood legends no amount of money could recreate.

### The Best Chef Awards
A global award for the people behind the food, voted on by a large body of chefs and food experts from around the world — and since half the voters are chefs themselves, it's essentially the profession grading its own, which chefs do not do gently. Founded in the mid-2010s, it moved away from a single ranked top-100 in 2024 to a tiered "knife" system — think stars, but sharper — while still crowning one chef The Best Chef each year. It's the clearest read available on who the people actually working the line most admire.

**Categories:**
- **Three Knives** — The top tier: chefs their own peers consider among the very best in the world.
- **Two Knives** — Chefs of outstanding international standing.
- **One Knife** — Excellence recognized on the world stage — a knife at any tier means the profession has taken notice.

### Spirited Awards
The bar world's most prestigious honors, presented since 2007 by the Tales of the Cocktail Foundation at its annual gathering in New Orleans — the industry's biggest event, where the world's best bartenders assemble in one famously thirsty city to judge each other. Winners are chosen through several rounds of voting by more than a hundred bartenders, writers, and drinks professionals. Where 50 Best captures buzz, the Spirited Awards measure something harder to fake: the deep respect of the people behind the bar.

**Categories:**
- **World's Best Bar** — The top international honor a bar can receive from its own industry — the toughest crowd there is.
- **Best US Bar / Best International Bar** — The finest bars in and outside the United States, judged separately.
- **Best New Bar** — The most impressive opening of the year, in US and international editions.
- **Best Cocktail Menu / Best Bar Team** — Craft-focused honors for the drinks list and the people who bring it to life.

### The Pinnacle Guide
The newest serious accolade in the bar world, founded by the team behind London Cocktail Week and often described as Michelin stars for cocktail bars — with the rigor to back the comparison up. Bars apply through a transparent process, are assessed across detailed criteria — the drinks, the hospitality, the room, staff welfare, sustainability — and then receive multiple anonymous in-person visits before any PIN is awarded. PINs are held for two years, after which a bar must earn them again. Only a small handful of the hundreds of pinned bars ever reach three — the guide sets that bar deliberately high and holds it there.

**Categories:**
- **One PIN** — An excellent cocktail bar, verified by anonymous reviewers.
- **Two PINs** — An outstanding cocktail bar — a clear cut above.
- **Three PINs** — An exceptional cocktail bar; the guide's highest and rarest honor, reached by only a select few of all pinned bars.

### OAD (Opinionated About Dining)
A restaurant ranking built from surveys of thousands of the world's most frequent fine diners — the people who fly to another country for lunch and consider it a reasonable use of a Tuesday. Each vote is weighted by how experienced the reviewer is, so the most traveled palates count the most. Because its voters eat everywhere, constantly, OAD often surfaces superb restaurants years before the bigger guides catch up — especially across Europe, where its lists carry real weight.

**Categories:**
- **Ranked lists (by region)** — Restaurants ranked numerically (e.g., "No. 42 · OAD") within regional lists such as the European Top 100+. The lower the number, the stronger the collective verdict of the world's most seasoned diners.

### La Liste
A French-born ranking, published annually since 2015, built on a simple idea: stop arguing about which guide is right and ask all of them at once. La Liste aggregates hundreds of guidebooks and millions of published reviews from around the world into a single score out of 100 per restaurant, then ranks the global top 1,000. A high score here means a restaurant excels by everyone's standards simultaneously — the closest thing food has to a unanimous decision.

**Categories:**
- **Score out of 100** — A restaurant's aggregate standing across the world's guides and reviews. The high 90s is where the arguments stop.

### World's 101 Best Steakhouses
The specialist authority on one gloriously focused question: where is the best steak on Earth? Founded in London in 2018, the organization sends anonymous inspectors to eat their way through every candidate, judging the quality and provenance of the meat, the range of cuts, the wine program, the service, and the room. It publishes an annual world ranking of 101 steakhouses plus regional lists — the map for when the mission is beef, and the mission is serious.

**Categories:**
- **Ranked 1–101** — A numerical world ranking; any spot on it marks one of the finest steakhouses on the planet, and the top ten are worth planning a trip around.

### Gault & Millau
France's other great restaurant guide, founded in 1965 by critics Henri Gault and Christian Millau, who thought French food had gotten heavy and said so loudly — championing the lighter, more inventive style that became nouvelle cuisine. Its anonymous reviewers score restaurants out of 20 and award one to five toques (chef's hats), with the focus squarely on the talent and creativity in the kitchen rather than the thickness of the carpet. Across France and much of Europe, a young chef's first toque is often the earliest reliable signal that a star is being born.

**Categories:**
- **Toques (1–5)** — Chef's hats tracking the kitchen's score out of 20. Four and five toques mark the very finest kitchens; a perfect 20 is nearly mythical.

### Tabelog
Japan's most trusted restaurant resource — a review platform used by millions of Japanese diners, with a famously strict algorithm that weights credible, experienced reviewers most heavily. Scores run out of 5, but the curve is merciless: crossing 3.5 already places a restaurant in the top few percent nationwide, and 4.0 is rare air. Its annual Tabelog Award — Gold, Silver, and Bronze tiers — distills the country's absolute best, and promptly makes them nearly impossible to book.

**Categories:**
- **Tabelog Award — Gold** — The top tier of Japan's annual best-restaurant selection; securing a table is a competitive sport.
- **Tabelog Award — Silver** — The second tier — still an elite national distinction.
- **Tabelog Award — Bronze** — The third tier, marking a restaurant among Japan's finest. In a country this good at food, that's saying plenty.

### Forbes Travel Guide
The company that invented the five-star rating, inspecting hotels and restaurants since 1958 — originally as the Mobil Travel Guide — and spending the decades since making sure the stars still mean something. Its professional inspectors arrive anonymously, pay their own way, and grade against hundreds of exacting, objective service standards, down to details you'd never consciously notice but would absolutely feel. Where most guides judge the food, Forbes measures the complete experience — so a Forbes star signals flawless hospitality from the moment the door opens.

**Categories:**
- **Five-Star** — A flawless, world-class experience; the guide's highest honor. The inspectors checked everything, and it all passed.
- **Four-Star** — Exceptional quality and service, just shy of the summit.
- **Recommended** — Consistently excellent establishments the inspectors are confident sending you to.

### Gambero Rosso
Italy's most influential food authority, publishing its restaurant guide since the 1980s — and when Italians rank Italian food, the rest of the world sensibly pays attention. Its critics score restaurants on a points scale, with the top honor, the Tre Forchette (Three Forks), serving as Italy's homegrown equivalent of three Michelin stars. Because the judges are Italian critics evaluating Italian cooking, many insiders consider it the truer measure of the country's kitchens.

**Categories:**
- **Tre Forchette (Three Forks)** — The guide's highest rating and Italy's most coveted domestic honor, awarded by the toughest possible audience.
- **Due Forchette / Una Forchetta (Two Forks / One Fork)** — Excellent and very good restaurants respectively, on Gambero Rosso's points scale.

### Guía Repsol
Spain's beloved national restaurant guide, awarding Soles (Suns) since 1979 — the Spanish counterpart to Michelin stars, judged by Spanish gastronomy experts who know exactly what a great tortilla should taste like. Three Soles carries enormous weight at home, and the guide has a particular knack for celebrating deeply Spanish cooking that international guides tend to drive right past.

**Categories:**
- **Tres Soles (Three Suns)** — The guide's highest honor, reserved for Spain's greatest restaurants.
- **Dos Soles / Un Sol (Two Suns / One Sun)** — Outstanding and excellent restaurants respectively.

### AA Rosettes
The United Kingdom and Ireland's longest-running restaurant award, given by the AA's professional inspectors since 1956 — yes, the roadside-assistance people, in a charming echo of Michelin's tire-company origins. Rosettes run from one to five, with four and five reserved for cooking that stands comparison with the best in the world. Five-Rosette restaurants are nearly as rare in Britain as three-star ones.

**Categories:**
- **Five / Four Rosettes** — Cooking among the very best in the British Isles, full stop.
- **Three Rosettes** — Outstanding restaurants worth traveling well out of your way for.
- **One–Two Rosettes** — Excellent restaurants achieving consistently high standards.

### Good Food Guide Chef Hats (Australia)
Australia's defining restaurant honor, awarded by the Good Food Guide's critics for decades. Restaurants earn one, two, or three Chef Hats — Australians treat hats precisely the way Europeans treat stars, arguments included — and a three-hatted restaurant sits at the very top of one of the world's most underrated dining scenes.

**Categories:**
- **Three Hats** — The pinnacle of Australian dining.
- **Two Hats** — Excellent restaurants of national standing.
- **One Hat** — A serious distinction marking one of the best rooms in its city.

### 50 Top Pizza
The world authority on pizza, published annually from Italy since 2017 — because if anyone was going to rank pizza with academic seriousness, it was always going to be the Italians. Anonymous inspectors judge everything from dough fermentation to service, producing ranked lists for Italy, the world, and major regions. A high placement has turned humble neighborhood pizzerias into international pilgrimage sites, lines included. *(Natural fit for the casual sister project.)*

**Categories:**
- **Ranked lists (World / Italy / regional)** — Numerical rankings of the best pizzerias on Earth; world No. 1 is pizza's most coveted title, and it is defended fiercely.

### AAA Diamonds
North America's long-running hospitality rating, with professional inspectors evaluating restaurants and hotels across the US, Canada, Mexico, and the Caribbean. Establishments earn from one to five Diamonds; Five Diamonds is the rarest tier, held by only a small fraction of one percent of everything AAA rates, and signals food and service operating at a world-class level.

**Categories:**
- **Five Diamond** — The rarest tier: leading-edge cuisine and service so attentive it borders on telepathy.
- **Four Diamond** — Distinctive fine dining with refined service and ambiance.

### Wine Spectator Restaurant Awards
The global standard for restaurant wine programs, awarded by Wine Spectator magazine since 1981. It judges the cellar rather than the kitchen — the perfect complement to food-first guides, and the reason sommeliers keep these plaques polished. Three tiers run from a well-chosen list to the Grand Award's vast, deep cellars; a Grand Award restaurant is a destination for the wine list every bit as much as the cooking.

**Categories:**
- **Grand Award** — The highest honor, for the world's greatest wine programs; only a very small number of restaurants worldwide hold it.
- **Best of Award of Excellence** — Extensive lists with real depth and breadth.
- **Award of Excellence** — Well-chosen lists that pair thoughtfully with the menu.
