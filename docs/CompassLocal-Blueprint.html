Compass Local — Launch Blueprint

The everyday sister to CompassEats. Award winners highlighted, not required. The great spot near you that you didn't know about.
v1.0 · July 16, 2026 · Written while the flagship punch list is still open — re-validate every assumption at kickoff

01The refined goal

CompassEats answers "which of the world's best is worth planning around?" Compass Local answers the argument people actually have most nights:

CompassEats — Charted

The best of the best. Reservations, occasions, trip anchors. Brass, Fraunces, "worth the detour." Every venue backed by a named award. Trust promise: nothing is here without a citation.

Compass Local — Loved

No plan, no reservation. "What's good near me? Is it open? Never heard of it — but it's clearly beloved. Let's go." Terracotta, looser voice. Trust promise: nothing is here by accident — every venue earned its spot, and we tell you how.

The Tuesday-night test

Every product decision gets checked against this scenario: it's 6:40pm, no reservation, you're in your own neighborhood or halfway through a three-week trip across Europe. You want quality without homework. If a feature doesn't serve that moment, it waits.

  • It is: global from day one · subdomain of the flagship (local.compasseats.com) · lists + map at launch · fast, simple, no accounts.
  • It is not: a Yelp clone (no crowd reviews as the ranking engine) · a diluted copy of the flagship · fine dining · a social app.
  • Success looks like: a user opens it cold in an unfamiliar city, finds a spot in under a minute, trusts why it's listed, and it's open.

02The trust model — two signal classes

The flagship's strict award-only rule doesn't fit casual dining: a 40-year-old neighborhood pizza joint may be as good as the 50 Top Pizza winner down the street and will never appear on a list. Compass Local admits two clearly-labeled classes, so the honesty promise survives the looser scope.

ClassBadge registerWhat earns itExamples
Charted award-backed Brass — same visual language as the flagship Named on an authoritative list or award, cited exactly like the flagship does it 50 Top Pizza · James Beard Outstanding Bakery · GABF / World Beer Cup medals · America's Classics
Institution beloved Terracotta — visually distinct, never imitates an award Meets a written, published rubric (the Institution Standard, task T2) — think longevity, sustained local devotion, recognition in respected editorial sources The 40-year corner slice shop · the classic café every guidebook to that city mentions
Honesty firewall, extended: the two classes never blur. An Institution badge must never look like an award. Every Charted venue names its source; every Institution venue states which rubric criteria it met. If a venue can't clear either bar, it doesn't exist on the site — "pretty good and nearby" is Google Maps' job, not ours.
Open question (do not decide until kickoff): whether public rating floors (e.g., sustained 4.5+ across a large review count) may serve as corroboration inside the Institution rubric. It can never be the sole qualifier — that's the line between "curated" and "scraped Yelp."

03Lanes & the launch gate

Six lanes, per the network-architecture doc. Rather than all-or-nothing, each lane ships when it clears a readiness gate — so a thin lane can't drag the launch or embarrass the brand.

Launch gate (proposed): a lane goes live when it has ≥150 venues across ≥3 countries (Institutions lane: ≥3 US regions until international rubric sources are proven). Numbers are placeholders to pressure-test at kickoff, not commitments.

06Ready-to-paste prompts (shelf-stable tasks)

These four survive being shelved because they don't depend on flagship outcomes. Copy, paste, send — each tells the model to ask for whichever project docs it needs.

T1 — The Source Atlas send to Fable · Research feature or Cowork

I run CompassEats, a restaurant and cocktail-bar discovery site where every venue is backed by a named culinary award (Michelin, World's 50 Best, James Beard — 26 sources total). I'm planning a sister site, Compass Local, covering casual venues globally in six lanes: pizza, bakeries/patisseries, coffee, breweries/taprooms, ice cream/gelato, and beloved neighborhood institutions. Before building anything I need to know whether authoritative sources actually exist to support each lane worldwide.

Build me a Source Atlas: for each of the six lanes, find the credible award programs, ranked lists, and inspection-based guides that honor individual visitable venues (not producers, not people) anywhere in the world. For each source report: what it is and who runs it, why it's credible, geographic coverage, how many venues per edition, update cadence, and how gettable the data is (structured web pages I could scrape or paste, PDFs, or manual-only). Skip pure crowd-review platforms — the whole premise is authority, not popularity.

Done means: one table per lane, then a verdict per lane — could this lane reach roughly 150 venues across 3+ countries on named sources alone, or will it depend heavily on a curated "local institution" tier we maintain ourselves? Close with a ranked recommendation of which lanes to launch first based purely on source strength. Flag any source whose licensing or terms would make republishing venue names risky. Check the atlas for dead or renamed programs before you finish — casual-food awards come and go faster than Michelin does.

T2 — The Institution Standard send to Fable · chat

I run CompassEats, a discovery site where every restaurant and cocktail bar is backed by a named award. I'm designing its casual sister site, Compass Local, which adds a second class of venue: the "Institution" — the beloved 40-year-old neighborhood spot that's as good as the award winner next door but will never appear on a list. This class needs a written standard rigorous enough that the site never drifts into being a Yelp clone; the standard is the product's integrity.

Draft the Institution Standard as a one-page document I can publish on the site's About page. It must define: the qualifying criteria (think dimensions like operating longevity, sustained local devotion, recognition in respected editorial or cultural sources — you decide the exact bar and defend it), what evidence is required to admit a venue and where that evidence must come from, and whether high public ratings may ever serve as corroboration — take a clear position on that with your reasoning, knowing my worry is that leaning on ratings at all erodes the "curated, not scraped" promise. Use James Beard's America's Classics criteria as a calibration reference for the spirit I'm after.

Include three worked examples of real venues that would qualify and three plausible near-misses that wouldn't, with one line each on why — invent none of them; verify every venue you name actually exists via search. Write the badge language a user sees on a venue page (one sentence, warm but honest, that could never be mistaken for an award citation). Before you finish, stress-test the standard: could a mediocre tourist trap with longevity slip through? If yes, tighten it and say what you changed.

T4 — Brand extension spec Fable for voice · Claude Design for visuals

I'm extending my brand. CompassEats is a restaurant/cocktail-bar discovery site with an editorial travel-journal aesthetic: dark charcoal #23211E and warm paper #F7F3EB dual modes, brass gold #C6A15B for fills, bronze #895F2E for accent text on light surfaces (brass fails contrast there — that lesson is settled, keep it), Fraunces display serif + Hanken Grotesk body, a compass-needle logo mark, voice that's knowledgeable and warm like a well-traveled friend. Its sister site, Compass Local, covers beloved casual spots — pizza, bakeries, coffee, breweries, gelato, neighborhood institutions — and per my brand-architecture plan it lives in a visibly different register: terracotta/clay-led (#D88A63 family) instead of brass-led, same typefaces, same compass mark family, voice one notch looser and quicker. Same compass, different needle: the flagship is "charted," Local is "loved."

Produce two deliverables. First, a design-token spec for Compass Local: dual-mode palette anchored on the clay family, with every text/background pairing contrast-checked to WCAG AA the way the flagship's brass/bronze split was — if clay fails contrast anywhere, derive a darker companion tone and name it, exactly parallel to the brass→bronze solution. Cover primary fills, accent text, both background modes, and state explicitly which flagship tokens carry over unchanged. Second, a one-page voice guide: three adjectives, three never-do rules, and five sample venue one-liners in the new register — one each for a pizza joint, a bakery, a coffee shop, a brewery taproom, and a 40-year diner. The one-liners should sound like a local friend texting you, not a critic filing a review, while never inventing facts about real venues — use fictional venue names.

Ask me to share the flagship design-system and brand-architecture docs before you start, and check every color pairing's contrast ratio arithmetic before you finish.

T7a — America's Classics gap years send to Sonnet · chat

I maintain a "Sister-Project Backlog" document for a future casual-dining site. It already holds James Beard America's Classics winners for 2018, 2019, and 2023, but the 2022, 2024, and 2025 slates were never pulled. Search jamesbeard.org and reputable coverage to compile the complete America's Classics winner lists for 2022, 2024, and 2025. For each venue give: exact venue name, city, state, and award year. Facts only from what you actually find via search — if a year's list can't be fully verified, say which venues are confirmed and which are uncertain rather than guessing. Output as one clean table sorted by year then venue name, ready for me to paste into the backlog doc, and note the total count per year so I can sanity-check completeness against the announcements.
