The Sister-Project Backlog

A holding pen for venues and award categories that belong to the future casual-dining project — not the flagship guide. So we never lose track of what we set aside.
v1.1 · Created July 1, 2026 · Updated July 18, 2026 · Seeded from the James Beard 2018–2024 ingest pass
The flagship CompassEats guide covers serious restaurants and cocktail bars ranked by the world's authoritative lists. The sister project is the eventual home for the more casual end — bakeries, pizza parlors, coffee shops, breweries, beloved neighborhood institutions, and casual full-service restaurants that don't demand a reservation or an occasion (the Bib Gourmand-equivalent tier: great, dependable, low-ceremony). When we're working through an award source and hit something that's real and prestigious but casual, it lands here instead of getting thrown away.

05Roadmap: casual categories to build out later

The stated shape of the sister project. When it launches, these are the lanes — and each will want its own authoritative sources the way the flagship uses Michelin, 50 Best, etc.

  • Bakeries & patisseries — seed list already in §01 above.
  • Pizza parlors — e.g. 50 Top Pizza (USA / World) as a source.
  • Coffee shops / roasters — specialty coffee guides, Coffee Review.
  • Breweries & taprooms — GABF / World Beer Cup medalists, RateBeer.
  • Beloved neighborhood institutions — America's Classics (§02) is a natural spine for this.
  • Casual full-service restaurants — the Bib Gourmand-equivalent tier: real cooking, don't always need a reservation, the kind of place a local just knows and loves. Sits below the flagship's fine-dining bar but well above "just fine." Wine Spectator's Award of Excellence tier is a confirmed source for this lane — see §06.
Scope clarified July 18, 2026: the sister project isn't defined only by venue type (bakery, pizza, coffee, brewery). It also covers a casual tier within full-service restaurants — the Bib Gourmand-equivalent level — that doesn't belong in the flagship's higher-ceremony curation but is exactly the kind of place a well-traveled friend would point you to on a Tuesday. Any award source with a clear "very good, dependable, no fuss" tier is worth checking against this lane, not just the four original categories.

06Wine Spectator — Award of Excellence confirmed source, not yet pulled

Wine Spectator's 2026 Restaurant Awards run three tiers: Grand Award (99) and Best of Award of Excellence (1,830) are prestigious enough for the flagship guide and are being ingested there. The base Award of Excellence tier (2,083 restaurants) is a different animal — "well-chosen wine list," not "destination restaurant" — and reads as exactly the casual, dependable, no-reservation-needed tier this project exists for.

Confirmed during the July 18, 2026 monthly Award Radar review, alongside the decision to keep the top two tiers in the flagship. Not pulled yet — deliberately.
  • Count: 2,083 restaurants, 2026 edition · all 50 US states + 87 countries/territories
  • Source: Wine Spectator Restaurant Search (winespectator.com/restaurants/search), filterable by award level
  • Why not pulled now: the list resets annually with real turnover. Scraping 2,083 rows today just to have them sit unused until this project launches means re-verifying stale data later anyway. Better to pull the then-current edition fresh when the Source Atlas phase (Compass Local Blueprint, Phase 1) actually kicks off.
  • When it's time: pull via the Restaurant Search tool, same ingest-template shape as other sources (name, city, country, award level). Guide-copy voice should match the "casual, dependable" register — different from the flagship's Wine Spectator Grand Award copy, which already exists in CompassEats-Guide-Descriptions.md.
